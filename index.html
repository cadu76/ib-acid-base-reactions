Syllabus Theory & Calculations (IB Topic 8 & 18)

1. Brønsted–Lowry Acid-Base Definition

A **Brønsted–Lowry acid** is a proton ($H^+$) donor, and a **Brønsted–Lowry base** is a proton acceptor. When an acid donor releases a proton, it forms its **conjugate base**. Conversely, when a base accepts a proton, it forms its **conjugate acid**.

HA (acid) + H₂O (base) ⇌ H₃O⁺ (conjugate acid) + A⁻ (conjugate base)

2. The Chemistry Engine: Unified Equilibrium Equations

The pH of an aqueous mixture of any acid (strong or weak) and base (strong or weak) is determined by enforcing water's autoionization constant ($K_w = [H^+][OH^-] = 10^{-14}$) and the charge neutrality condition.

Charge Neutrality Condition
[H⁺] + [BH⁺] = [OH⁻] + [A⁻]
The total positive charges from protons and conjugate bases must equal the total negative charges from hydroxides and acid anions.
General Solver Formula
x + C_b(x / (x + K_a^BH)) - K_w/x - C_a(K_a^HA / (x + K_a^HA)) = 0
Where $x = [H^+]$, $C_a$ and $C_b$ are analytical concentrations after dilution, and $K_a^{BH} = K_w / K_b$.

3. Key Titration Milestones

titrations contain distinct stages that are frequently assessed on the IB Paper 2 exams:

Half-Equivalence Point (Weak-Strong only)
pH = pKₐ (at 50% neutralization)
At this volume, half of the weak acid has been converted to its salt conjugate base. Thus $[HA] = [A^-]$. Substituting this into the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation ($pH = pK_a + \log\frac{[A^-]}{[HA]}$) simplifies to $pH = pK_a$. This acts as a chemical buffer.
Equivalence Point & Salt Hydrolysis
SA-SB: pH = 7 | WA-SB: pH > 7 | SA-WB: pH < 7
For weak-strong systems, the salt formed undergoes **hydrolysis**. For example, in a Weak Acid titration, the conjugate base $A^-$ reacts with water: $A^- + H_2O \rightleftharpoons HA + OH^-$, generating hydroxide and making the equivalence pH basic.

4. Conductivity Titrations

Electrical conductivity depends on the concentration and mobility of ions present. Protons ($H^+$) and hydroxide ions ($OH^-$) have anomalously high molar ionic conductivities due to the Grotthuss proton-hopping mechanism in water.

  • **Strong Acid + Strong Base**: Conductivity starts very high ($H^+, Cl^-$), drops linearly to a minimum at the equivalence point where $H^+$ is fully neutralized to form neutral water (leaving only $Na^+, Cl^-$), and then rises steeply as excess mobile $OH^-$ is added.
  • **Weak Acid + Strong Base**: Conductivity starts very low (weak acid is poorly dissociated). As base is added, conductivity increases slowly as neutral $HA$ is replaced by mobile salt ions ($Na^+, A^-$). After the equivalence point, the conductivity rises sharply due to highly conductive excess $OH^-$.
